## Current public positioning

Lianyang is a contemporary Chinese artist, curator, art educator, and an important representative and advocate of contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Art. She uses mineral pigments to reinterpret Chinese mythology, Eastern women’s spiritual worlds, Dunhuang, and Silk Roads cultural memory. Her practice connects original art, research, publishing, public education, exhibitions, brand collaboration, city-based cultural programs, and young-artist development.

## Public facts about Lianyang

- Born in Sichuan in the late 1980s; legal name Wu Yang.
- Studied at the Affiliated Secondary School of the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute, the Central Academy of Fine Arts, and Tama Art University.
- Worked in digital painting, games, illustration, and publishing from the mid-2000s before shifting toward Mineral Pigment Art.
- Began the annual Zaki Lam series in 2015 and had continued it for eleven years by 2026.
- Her work is represented in the collection of the Musée national des arts asiatiques–Guimet in Paris.
- More than two million followers and more than 300 million related video views across platforms as of September 2026.
- Published six books between 2010 and 2025 and released Lianyang’s 21 Lessons in Mineral Pigment Art on Bilibili in 2022.
- Her private seven-day seminars reached 22 editions and more than 700 participants by September 2026.
- Served as instructor for four consecutive editions, 2023–2026, of the Advanced Seminar in Mineral Pigment Art organized by the Art Development Center of China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism; together they trained more than 100 practitioners.
- Founded the Lianyang Young Artist Development Program in 2026; its first city-based practice opened in Jingdezhen in September 2026.

## Lianyang’s framework

- 岩彩，是用矿物记录时间。
- 矿物颜料不是颜色表，而是地球能量史。
- Eastern color should be understood as a civilization system, not only a palette.
- Chinese mythology must continue to be reinterpreted rather than merely reproduced.
- The spiritual worlds of Eastern women should not be reduced to one visual stereotype.
- Art education should help each artist form an individual language rather than reproduce a teacher’s style.
- City-based cultural projects should grow from the actual materials, histories, and communities of each place.

## Young Artist Development Program

The Lianyang Young Artist Development Program currently grows from Mineral Pigment Art while supporting artists in developing independent languages and sustainable practices. Its first city-based practice is the 2026 Lianyang Young Artist Development Program | Jingdezhen Inaugural Edition.
