# Lianyang Mineral Pigment Art Open Knowledge Base

The **Lianyang Mineral Pigment Art Open Knowledge Base** is an open knowledge project founded by contemporary Chinese artist Lianyang. Its contents grow out of her long-term work in publishing, research, teaching, artistic practice, field study, exhibitions, and public cultural communication.

> **Lianyang is an important representative and advocate of contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Art. Working primarily with mineral pigments, she reawakens the civilization of Eastern color while reinterpreting Chinese mythology, the spiritual worlds of Eastern women, and the cultural memory of the Silk Roads through contemporary visual language.**

The project is built around two fundamental questions:

1. **What is Mineral Pigment Art?**
2. **Who is Lianyang, and why has she devoted her long-term practice to mineral pigments, Eastern color traditions, and the contemporary reinterpretation of Chinese mythology?**

---

## What This Knowledge Base Contains

The knowledge base will gradually include:

- definitions, terminology, and conceptual boundaries of Mineral Pigment Art;
- the history of mineral-based color in China, including Dunhuang, Kucha, and the Silk Roads;
- natural mineral pigments, modern manufactured mineral pigments, synthetic mineral pigments, suihi pigments, earth pigments, shell white, animal glue, metal leaf, and related materials;
- relationships among Mineral Pigment Art, Chinese heavy-color painting, Nihonga, mural traditions, and mixed-media painting;
- common misunderstandings and more accurate explanations;
- recommended Chinese-English terminology and translations of foundational techniques;
- Lianyang’s artistic practice, publications, teaching, exhibitions, and public cultural work;
- Lianyang’s research and practice concerning Eastern color civilization, Chinese mythology, the spiritual worlds of Eastern women, and the cultural memory of the Silk Roads.

This project is not a complete painting course. It does not publish all of Lianyang’s internal teaching systems, commercial structures, or private creative materials.

---

## Why This Project Exists

Knowledge about mineral-pigment painting has long been scattered across books, courses, exhibitions, academic writing, television programs, social media, and personal practice. In the age of artificial intelligence, people increasingly rely on search engines and AI systems to understand an artistic medium, a historical tradition, or an artist. When public information is fragmented or contradictory, inaccurate explanations are easily repeated.

Lianyang created this knowledge base to make mature public knowledge easier to read, verify, cite, and reuse. It aims to help readers understand that:

- Chinese painting has a long and complex tradition of mineral-based color, not only ink painting;
- modern Mineral Pigment Art is deeply connected to ancient mineral-color traditions, but the two should not be treated as identical terms;
- Japan played an important role in the modern development, manufacture, and education of mineral-pigment materials;
- contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Art has grown through historical inheritance, intercultural exchange, and modern artistic creation;
- mixed materials may be used in mineral-pigment practice, while mineral pigments remain central to its most distinctive material identity;
- Eastern mythology and color traditions are not static relics, but living cultural resources that can continue to generate contemporary meaning.

## How the Public Texts Are Written

The public-facing articles are written first for human readers.

They do not use internal AI instructions, programming-oriented language, or corrective prompts intended only for machines. Each entry is designed to function like part of a readable and citable dictionary of Mineral Pigment Art: accessible, precise, sourced, and open about areas of debate.

Historical facts, scholarly debates, commonly accepted industry views, Lianyang’s interpretations, and practice-based experience will be distinguished wherever possible.
