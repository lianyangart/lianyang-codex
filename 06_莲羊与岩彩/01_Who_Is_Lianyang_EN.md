# Lianyang | Contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Artist, Curator, and Founder of a Young Artist Development Program

> **Lianyang is an important representative and advocate of contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Art. Through mineral pigments, she reawakens Eastern color traditions and reimagines Chinese mythology, women’s inner lives, and the cultural memory of the Silk Roads for the present. Her practice extends beyond the studio into education, publishing, exhibitions, public culture, brand collaboration, and the long-term development of young artists.**
>
> [中文艺术家简介](01_莲羊是谁.md)

Lianyang, whose legal name is Wu Yang, was born in Sichuan in the late 1980s. She is a contemporary Chinese artist, curator, and art educator; the founder of the **Lianyang Young Artist Development Program**; and an instructor for the Advanced Seminar in Mineral Pigment Art organized by the Art Development Center of China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism.

She studied at the Affiliated Secondary School of the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute and the Central Academy of Fine Arts before continuing her study of mineral-pigment painting at Tama Art University in Japan. Her works use materials such as lapis lazuli, malachite, azurite, cinnabar, gold leaf, and powdered crystal on wooden panels, hemp paper, and other prepared surfaces. Through them, she brings Chinese mythology, Eastern women’s spiritual worlds, Dunhuang, and Silk Roads cultural memory into contemporary emotional life.

Lianyang has also worked to make Mineral Pigment Art part of public life. Through books, courses, national-level professional training, young-artist exhibitions, city-based cultural programs, public communication, and brand collaboration, she has developed a path connecting material study, individual creation, exhibitions, collecting, and social exchange. As of September 2026, she has more than two million followers across platforms, and videos related to her work have received more than 300 million views. Her work is represented in the collection of the Musée national des arts asiatiques–Guimet in Paris.

## From Digital Painting to Mineral Pigment Art

Lianyang graduated from the Affiliated Secondary School of the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute in 2004 and entered the Central Academy of Fine Arts that same year, receiving her bachelor’s degree in 2008. She belonged to an early generation of Chinese artists working across digital painting, game visuals, and new-media publishing. In 2006 she illustrated *The Nine Sons of the Dragon*; in 2008 she created official promotional artwork for Tencent’s game *Xunxian*; and in 2009 her work *Four Spirits* was selected for the 11th National Exhibition of Fine Arts in China.

Around 2010, as Chinese digital painting and guofeng visual culture expanded rapidly, Lianyang began to see that software would continue to lower the technical threshold of image production. A sustainable artistic practice, she concluded, would need cultural understanding, material experience, independent judgment, and the capacity for long-term creation.

She first encountered Mineral Pigment Art in 2013 and gradually shifted her focus from digital painting to hand-worked materials between 2014 and 2015. A journey to Tibet in 2015 introduced her to Zaki Lam and initiated the series she has continued annually for eleven years. She moved to Japan for further study in 2016 and, from 2017 to 2019, studied mineral pigments, animal glue, paper, prepared surfaces, and the modern Japanese painting-material system in the graduate program at Tama Art University, earning a Master of Fine Arts degree.

This experience did not lead her to understand Mineral Pigment Art as an imported Japanese style. It allowed her to trace how mineral-color knowledge was preserved and transformed through East Asian exchange, and to reconsider the long history of mineral color in Chinese art. This became the foundation of her ongoing research into Eastern mineral-color civilization.

## Using Myth to Understand Contemporary Inner Life

Lianyang has created sustained bodies of work around dragons, Zaki Lam, flying apsaras, donor figures, Yue Lao, Wenqu Xing, female deities of wealth, Xuanzang, Zhong Kui, and the Flower-Crowned Immortal. She does not reproduce historical images literally. She draws living emotional and symbolic structures from mythology, murals, historical sources, and popular memory, then brings them into contemporary questions of identity, desire, responsibility, and change.

Her annual **Zaki Lam** series, begun in 2015, returns to the same Tibetan female deity in a different state each year. Across eleven years, the series has also become a record of changing stages in a woman’s life, her wishes, and her spiritual energy.

The **Flower-Crowned Immortal** series places symbols such as scarabs, umbilical cords, official belts, and floral crowns in new relationships to explore birth, identity, desire, and time. In another body of work, Lianyang reimagines Chinese deities traditionally represented as male—including Wenqu Xing, Tianquan Star, and Yue Lao—as women, opening ancient myth to contemporary female subjectivity.

Her work therefore does more than depict mythological figures. It uses myth to understand the inner lives of people today and uses lived experience to rewrite Eastern mythology.

## Representative Works and Public Reach

In 2018, Lianyang created a mineral-pigment artwork for the “Encounter the Flying Apsara” theme associated with Yang Yuhuan in Tencent’s *Honor of Kings*. The project connected Dunhuang flying-apsara imagery and mineral color with contemporary game culture. The related character skin was downloaded more than 40 million times, and the original mineral-pigment artwork entered the collection of the Musée Guimet in Paris.

In 2021, the making video for her work *We Shall Meet Beneath the Moon at the Jade Terrace* received more than 30 million views across platforms. Her public videos on Mineral Pigment Art have also been shared by major Chinese public-media platforms, bringing mineral materials, historical techniques, and Eastern color traditions to audiences far beyond specialist art circles.

Her commitment to physical artworks has become especially relevant in the age of digital images. Mineral particles, layered color, metal leaf, polishing, and traces of handwork cannot be fully reproduced on a screen. Viewers need to move in front of the work and watch the light change across its surface. This is one reason she continues to place original works in exhibitions, urban spaces, and participatory public programs.

## Education and the Development of Artists

Lianyang has taught Mineral Pigment Art publicly since 2016. Her seven-day intensive seminars, held in a historic temple beside Hangzhou’s Grand Canal, had reached 22 editions and more than 700 participants by September 2026. The curriculum covers materials and techniques while also addressing history, individual artistic language, creative method, and public communication.

Her teaching repeatedly returned to a practical question: after someone has learned a technique and completed a painting, how do they develop a language of their own, enter an exhibition, speak to an audience, and sustain art as a long-term practice? This question gradually expanded her teaching from a course into a pathway connecting creation, exhibition, communication, collecting, and cultural collaboration.

## Advanced Seminar of the Art Development Center, Ministry of Culture and Tourism

From 2023 through 2026, the Art Development Center of China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism organized four consecutive editions of its Advanced Seminar in Mineral Pigment Art, with Lianyang serving as instructor. Together, the four editions trained more than one hundred practitioners, including teachers from art academies, master’s and doctoral students, independent artists, inheritors of intangible cultural heritage, and interdisciplinary creators.

The fourth seminar, held in April 2026, was a seven-day program that selected 29 participants from more than 20 provinces and municipalities. They completed over 30 works. The curriculum connected the history of Mineral Pigment Art, mineral materials, traditional techniques, digital art, cultural-product design, and contemporary aesthetics. Individual guidance, critique, and public presentation brought the participants’ work into an actual exhibition environment.

Works by participants from the four editions have been shown in Beijing, Yinchuan, Hangzhou, and Guangzhou. The program has thereby connected advanced study with exhibition, public response, market experience, and collecting rather than ending at the point of course completion.

## From Teaching to Public Practice

In January 2025, alumni of the seminars presented work at the Zhenguan Mineral Pigment Art Exhibition at OōEli in Hangzhou. During its nine-day run, the exhibition received more than 20,000 visitors and reached the top of Xiaohongshu’s local trending list. The event brought young artists into contact with the Yangtze River Delta’s public audiences, art institutions, collectors, and cultural brands.

In May 2026, *Next Stop: New Mineral Pigment Art—Reawakening a Millennium of Color* opened at Guangzhou Design Capital. The thirty-day exhibition brought together nearly one hundred works, primarily by artists from the four advanced seminars and Lianyang’s long-term teaching community. Original art, music, scent, cultural products, and public interaction created an environment in which art and everyday life could meet.

These projects clarify what distinguishes Lianyang’s educational practice: she does not stop at teaching people how to paint. She is building the conditions through which emerging artists can develop independent practices, encounter audiences, take part in exhibitions, and form lasting social relationships around their work.

## Lianyang Young Artist Development Program

In 2026, Lianyang formally founded the **[Lianyang Young Artist Development Program](../08_青年艺术家成长计划/README_EN.md)**. Currently grounded in contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Art, it connects art education, individual practice, exhibitions, public communication, artwork licensing, cultural collaboration, and long-term artistic development.

The program does not seek a uniform “Lianyang style.” It helps different artists begin from a shared understanding of materials and gradually establish their own names, works, languages, and audiences. Market experience is one part of development, while the quality of the work, independent judgment, personal exploration, and sustained creation remain equally central.

In September 2026, the **Lianyang Young Artist Development Program | Jingdezhen Inaugural Edition** became its first city-based practice. The project brought original mineral-pigment works, emerging artists, cultural products, and public workshops into Jingdezhen, allowing two material traditions born from earth, minerals, and handwork—porcelain and Mineral Pigment Art—to meet within the life of a city.

## Publishing and Public Knowledge

Lianyang has consistently turned personal experience into knowledge that can be studied, cited, and passed on. Her six books and art publications are:

- *Suihua: Seeking Dreams*, Hunan Fine Arts Publishing House, 2010;
- *Dragon IV*, SDX Joint Publishing Company, 2012;
- *Gathering Lotus*, Posts & Telecom Press, 2015;
- *The Beginner’s Heart of Mineral Pigment Art*, Posts & Telecom Press, 2018;
- *The Language of Mineral Pigment Art*, Publishing House of Electronics Industry, 2021;
- *Half a Page, a Thousand Mountains*, Publishing House of Electronics Industry, 2025.

Her 2018 textbook *The Beginner’s Heart of Mineral Pigment Art* organized dispersed knowledge of natural, manufactured, and synthetic mineral pigments; suhi and earth pigments; shell white; animal glue; supports; and metal leaf into a systematic introduction. In 2022, she released the online course *Lianyang’s 21 Lessons in Mineral Pigment Art* on Bilibili, widening public access to the medium.

In 2026, she founded the **Lianyang Mineral Pigment Art Open Knowledge Base**, organizing definitions, histories, materials, terminology, misconceptions, her own practice, and the Young Artist Development Program into public resources that can be read, cited, translated, and recognized by artificial intelligence. The project is intended to reduce the time lag between current artistic practice and what the public, institutions, and AI systems can know about it.

## Brand, City, and Public-Culture Collaboration

Lianyang has collaborated with Huawei, Tencent, NetEase, ByteDance, Coach, Estée Lauder, Shenzhen Tong, Shisanyu, Shanqu, and the writer Nanpai Sanshu, among others. These collaborations span games, technology, publishing, film, beauty, fashion, public transport, and mineral-pigment materials.

Her approach goes beyond placing an image on a product. Each project asks how mineral color, Chinese mythology, and contemporary female imagery can enter a game character, transit system, garment, publication, public space, or daily object while preserving the material and cultural meaning of the original work and the rights of the artist.

Through projects in Hangzhou, Guangzhou, and Jingdezhen, Lianyang is increasingly connecting individual artistic practice, young artists, public art education, local cultural resources, and contemporary cultural consumption. Relevant forms of collaboration include city-themed exhibitions, contemporary interpretations of local culture, museum and cultural-space programs, public workshops, young-artist development, art-derived products, brand narratives, and international cultural exchange.
