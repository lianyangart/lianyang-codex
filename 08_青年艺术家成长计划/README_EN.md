# Lianyang Young Artist Development Program

## From Learning to Independent Practice

> Founded by Lianyang<br>
> Current version: V1.1 | September 2026<br>
> [中文](README.md)

The **Lianyang Young Artist Development Program** was founded by contemporary Chinese artist Lianyang. Currently grounded in contemporary Chinese Mineral Pigment Art, it connects art education, individual practice, exhibitions, public communication, and cultural collaboration. Its purpose is to help young artists move from learning toward independent artistic practice.

The program grows from more than twenty years of Lianyang’s work as an artist and educator, and from a question that has repeatedly emerged in her teaching: once a person has learned to paint and completed a body of work, how can they continue to form their own judgment, language, and direction, and make art a practice they can sustain over time?

## More Than Twenty Years of Practice

Lianyang studied at the Affiliated Secondary School of the Sichuan Fine Arts Institute, the Central Academy of Fine Arts, and Tama Art University in Japan. Her practice has moved across digital painting, ink, illustration, and Mineral Pigment Art, while also extending into publishing, game art, art education, exhibition curation, and public cultural communication.

Chinese mythology, the spiritual worlds of Eastern women, historical figures, and the cultural memory of the Silk Roads have remained important subjects throughout these changes. Her media have evolved, but she has continued to seek a visual language capable of carrying Eastern subjects, personal experience, and contemporary feeling.

Moving from digital technology back to paper, brushes, animal glue, mineral pigments, and time-intensive work by hand required her to set aside familiar methods and begin again with materials. Field study in Dunhuang, Kucha, and other locations, together with sustained research into ancient murals, mineral materials, and Eastern visual traditions, gradually became part of both her work and her teaching.

These experiences shaped the program’s central understanding: artistic development depends on more than the ability to paint. Tools, forms of communication, and social conditions change. What supports a lasting practice also includes independent judgment, an individual artistic language, cultural understanding, and the ability to continue working over time.

## From Teaching to Long-Term Development

Through teaching, Lianyang has met artists at many stages. Some are still searching for materials and a direction of their own. Some can already make accomplished work but have little experience with exhibitions, artist statements, or public exchange. Others have worked professionally for years and are reconsidering their artistic path.

These questions cannot be resolved by a single technical demonstration. Learning how to introduce a work, take part in an exhibition, communicate with audiences and collectors, and approach licensing and collaboration requires experience in actual projects.

The program therefore extends its attention beyond the classroom. Through exhibitions, public exchange, city-based activities, artwork licensing, and cultural collaboration, it gives young artists opportunities to understand how art enters public life and how practice develops through response, responsibility, and sustained work.

## Developing an Individual Artistic Language

At present, Mineral Pigment Art provides a shared material foundation, but the program does not seek a uniform style. Each artist has a different relationship to subject matter, color, composition, and lived experience. Learning materials and techniques expands the possibilities of expression; understanding tradition creates a foundation for making new work in the present.

Lianyang has long emphasized that teaching should not produce students who reproduce her style. Its purpose is to help different artists establish their own mineral-pigment languages. A teacher can offer experience, and a team or project can provide support, but the work and the path ultimately belong to the artist.

Development here means beginning with shared learning, then gradually establishing one’s own name, work, language, and audience, and ultimately becoming an artist capable of moving forward independently.

## Bringing Art into Public Life

When work leaves the studio and enters an exhibition, artists encounter responses beyond the classroom: where visitors pause, what questions they ask, how they understand a work, and why they may wish to learn more or collect it.

These responses help artists understand how their work communicates and introduce the responsibilities of artistic collaboration. The market is one part of this experience, but it is not the only measure. The quality of the work, individual exploration, and continuity of practice remain equally important.

Artwork licensing and cultural collaboration raise further questions. When an image moves onto a different material or into daily life, which qualities of the original should be preserved? How should an artist understand production, context, and copyright? Through this process, a work may enter contemporary life in forms beyond the exhibition wall.

## Grounded in Mineral Pigment Art, Open to the Future

The program currently begins with the area in which Lianyang has built her deepest body of creative, research, and teaching experience: Mineral Pigment Art. It first serves emerging mineral-pigment artists who are developing individual languages of their own.

Its long-term direction is broader than the reproduction of a single medium or style. The program connects material research, cultural understanding, independent creation, and public practice. As it develops, it may also form new relationships with other artistic media and cities while preserving this core method. Any expansion will grow from the real needs of artists and their work.

## Jingdezhen Inaugural Edition

In 2026, the **Lianyang Young Artist Development Program | Jingdezhen Inaugural Edition** became the program’s first city-based practice.

Jingdezhen has a deep ceramic tradition and remains a place where materials, craft, and contemporary life meet. Porcelain and Mineral Pigment Art both arise from earth, minerals, and human hands. One takes form through fire; the other unfolds as color in painting. Their meeting creates a distinctive setting for exhibitions, public exchange, and exploration across materials.

The Jingdezhen edition consists of four parts:

1. **Original Mineral-Pigment Paintings (岩彩原作):** Original works by Lianyang and young artists, allowing visitors to see the textures created by mineral particles, layered color, and metal materials.
2. **New Voices in Mineral Pigment Art (岩彩新生计划):** Exhibitions and public exchange that support emerging mineral-pigment artists as they continue creating and develop individual languages.
3. **Mineral Creations Studio (矿物造物所):** Artist-authorized works and mineral aesthetics explored in forms that can enter contemporary life.
4. **Mineral Time Workshop (矿物时间工坊):** Hands-on experiences that introduce the materials, labor, and time behind a mineral-pigment painting.

Jingdezhen is a beginning rather than an endpoint. Responses from audiences, exhibitions, and collaboration will continue to inform future city-based programs and the long-term development of young artists.

## Ancient Materials, a New Generation, and New Technology

Digital tools and artificial intelligence are changing both creation and communication. Lianyang continues to ask how artists can form their own questions, aesthetic judgment, and expression when technology can help people accomplish more.

Tools can be learned and used, but firsthand experience, material practice, cultural understanding, and independent judgment still require the artist’s direct engagement. The program returns these questions to particular people and works: learning through materials, making choices through creation, encountering the public through exhibitions, and understanding the world through collaboration.

More than twenty years of Lianyang’s experience thus becomes a foundation on which another generation can build.
